This story goes back even before I was born. My mother used to practice meditation a lot in those days and used to go on 10-day-long sessions where they go to a dedicated location to practice. In Buddhist philosophy, it's considered a way to reach nirvana, I guess. My mother had some experiences during meditation, and according to the great practitioners, they said it was a good thing, and that experiencing all that stuff meant she was on the right track towards nirvana. I’m not much of a believer, but I have studied and experienced enough through meditation to make me believe in a single source of energy that keeps everything going.
When I was born, my mother had almost stopped meditating, but she used to meditate when she was pregnant with me. Then, somehow in my early childhood, I also had a strong urge to practice meditation. Nobody in our house showed as much interest as I did. My mother had also restarted her practices during those days, so I used to sit with her. Eventually, I learned the right way to meditate and different methods to evoke different experiences.
There’s a study which says that if your mind is not strong or not in a good state, you shouldn’t practice serious meditation. Mindfulness is good, but not the intense kind. Up until 10th grade, I was completely fine in body and mind, but everything shifted for me mentally during and after that year. Apparently, the shifts were symptoms of the stress and psychological load I’d been carrying since my childhood unknowingly.
After 10th grade, my meditation practices became rigorous. Everything went fine until I left for Sangli. I hadn’t stopped meditating when I got there. The first room I got in Sangli was horrible. An old couple owned the property, and it looked straight out of a horror movie. But it didn’t affect me much at first; I never cared because I never believed. After a month or two, however, my mental health started declining more quickly than I cared to notice. I was completely clueless as to why it was happening to me, mainly because I was skeptical about depression and the reality of mental illness. I just didn't believe in it.
During those months, I started having bad experiences in that house. Things were already difficult because of my roommate, the owners, the mental decline, and the college load.
Another important thing I should document is that since my early childhood, I had been seeing auras, which appeared as real light of different colors flaming around people. I started seeing these things around 4th grade, I guess, and I used to think it was nothing. I never knew why, or maybe I chose not to believe in the religious or spiritual explanations. I chose to believe it was just my migraines. Back then, my migraine attacks were pretty severe; they would last for days, and I couldn’t stand to see light during them.
Back to the story: I had my first actual bad experience when I was going to the washroom around midnight. I still get chills writing this. The washroom wasn't in the room; it was out in the back of the house. I was walking, got sudden chills, and chose to ignore them. I felt like something was staring at me in the dark, but I ignored that too. After using the washroom, as I started walking back towards my room, I saw two things. I don’t know what they were. They appeared as dark figures, one short and one tall. It scared the shit out of me, and I ran as fast as I could back to the room. I told my roommate, and he admitted, "Yes, I too sometimes get scared around here."
I couldn’t sleep properly that night. I started looking for ways to figure out what I saw. That's when I realized there’s a specific meditation practice called astral projection. Again, to clarify, I’m not a believer. I don’t even know why I was so into meditating to begin with, maybe it was passed down from my mother. Regardless, I started practicing this technique. I won't write down all the details here. Usually, the expected results take a lot of practice, and a part of me still thinks it's all just a mind game.
But here is what happened to me. The premise is that while your eyes are closed, you can see exactly what’s happening around you and move without moving your physical body. I know it sounds like complete nonsense, and part of me still thinks it is, but I am just recording my experience. After many days of practicing this at night before sleeping, I had my final experience with it, and I stopped practicing completely after this day.
My eyes were closed. It felt like a dream, but I was awake. I was meditating, and I felt my legs moving and levitating in the air. It wasn’t happening physically, of course, but I felt it. I could see my body while my eyes were closed. I saw what I was wearing, and I saw my roommate doing his work. My hands and legs felt suspended in the air. I started to move from my place, like my astral body was moving, or more accurately, like my perspective was teleporting from place to place. Just writing this gives me goosebumps.
I saw myself out of the room, and those two figures were there. I was looking at them, and they were looking at me. It terrified me, but I couldn’t get back to my senses. Those figures were terrifying. Somehow, I forced my eyes open. I was so scared.
After that, the semester ended, and I went back home. By that time, my parents knew something was wrong with me, though I hadn’t shared anything about what happened in Sangli. My sister decided to get someone to do a pooja at home. A woman came over, nobody knew her, and she certainly didn't know us. She was simple, and her way of doing pooja was different. She was meditating. Nobody does this, but she did. She asked my sister to sit in silence or meditate with her, and I decided to join in and meditate as well.
As I started, I found myself getting distracted. It felt like a woman was distracting me; she felt powerful, but I told myself it was all my imagination. When the woman finished her meditation, the very first thing she did was point at me and say to everyone that I was not okay, that there was something wrong with me. I was thinking, what the hell is she talking about? Then she started explaining. She said that while she was meditating, she was being distracted by a woman. That blew my mind, because the exact same thing had just happened to me.
She said that a bad energy was around me, trying to attach itself. She even stated that I didn't live here, that all my problems were stemming from the city and the place where I lived, and that I wasn't safe there, or even in this house, because it wasn't protected. She explained all this not in a religious way, but in a surprisingly rational way. I found myself understanding exactly what she meant, and I could feel that she was right.
After going back to Sangli, I left that room. Everything felt normal again. I was feeling okay.
What I was entirely unaware of was the knowledge that someone who isn't mentally healthy shouldn't meditate, because the world we see isn’t as simple as it appears. I'm talking about energies present as waveforms, rays, or other natural forces, not movie bullshit. When you’re mentally vulnerable, you can attract bad energies, making yourself even sicker. Dark energies can latch onto weak minds, making them prone to breaking down.
When I went to my first therapy session after my first attempt, I told the therapist all of this, things I had never shared with anyone. I told him about the auras, the meditation, everything. He explained it all back to me through the lens of science. That’s when I realized how deep this really goes. He asked me to stop meditating altogether. He told me not to practice any techniques, that I could try basic mindfulness, but for now, I needed to give up everything else for a few months, maybe a year or two, until things stabilized. I finally understood why he said that.
After coming back to Nagpur, our family doctor, who is excellent, told me the exact same thing: stop meditating.
My parents don’t know about all of this. Very few people know about the auras. My cousin knows, and now I’m finally cementing it here in my own journal. There’s so much more, but most of it I choose not to dig up right now. I’ll write it out when I feel I have enough capacity in me.
After all that, things normalized. I was fine, and nothing like that ever happened to me again. And I guess after that I met Vedant, and things just took a turn from there.

It hit me today. I'm not just reacting to what's happening right now with Ganisha. A massive part of me is just playing out a script my body memorized back in my first relationship. Somewhere down the line my nervous system drew up this rigid blueprint of what love is supposed to look like. It memorized how often we should talk, what reassurance feels like, how affection is proved. The terrifying part is that my body doesn't view these as just preferences. It treats them like absolute survival requirements. When things deviate from that old script my body hijacks everything before my logical brain can catch up. My chest tightens and my thoughts go entirely off the rails. I start frantically grasping for reassurance. It feels like the floor is caving in even when absolutely nothing is actually wrong.
The crazy thing is that the rational part of me knows this. I know Ganisha isn't my past. She's her own person. She has her own messy and unique way of loving, communicating, and processing things. Expecting her to fit perfectly into a mold shaped by someone else is completely unfair to her and to me. But there's this stubborn and scared part of me still waiting for the old patterns to show up. It just confuses familiarity with safety. But here's the other thing. I can't write off everything I feel as just my anxiety acting up. I have real needs too. Needs that aren't ghosts from the past but just who I am right now. I genuinely need a dynamic where we can talk about the heavy and awful stuff without this looming terror that she's going to walk away. I want us to be able to say we're hurting without it turning into a war. To just keep choosing each other even when it's deeply uncomfortable. I don't give a damn about perfection anymore. I just need to know we're willing to grow and figure it out together. So I'm sitting here holding two very different truths. One is that my nervous system is sometimes just begging for reassurance because it's trying to resurrect a feeling of safety from a past life. Two is that I have valid and breathing emotional needs that deserve space. They shouldn't just be shoved into the anxiety box and ignored. It's not my job to force Ganisha into an old mold. My actual work is learning to pause and ask myself if I'm asking for this right now because I'm terrified. Or am I asking because I genuinely crave connection? Those two things might look similar but they couldn't be more different. What if I stop trying to find someone who fits the exact shape of my old scars. Maybe love is just building a house from the dirt up. Just the two of us making our own rules. I am not going to pretend I don't need things. I am still going to take up space. I just have to catch myself when the panic starts talking. I have to figure out what is just pure fear and what is actual love. And then I have to open my mouth and say it. I can't let anxiety be the loudest voice in the room anymore.
